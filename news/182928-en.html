Pinghan Liang presents research on environmental awareness and local land revenue

2023-05-10

English translation of the original Chinese article.

On April 24, at the invitation of SUSTech's prospective Department of Applied Economics and Associate Professor Maoliang Ye, Professor Pinghan Liang of Sun Yat-sen University presented an online seminar, ‘The Art of Not Being Chocked: Environmental Awareness, Vote with Feet, and Land Finance in China.’ Ye chaired the event. Liang, an economics PhD from the Autonomous University of Barcelona and a 2022–23 Swire visiting scholar at Oxford's St Antony's College, studies local government behavior, behavioral and experimental economics, and the social effects of digital technology.

Liang explained that Chinese local governments rely heavily on revenue from land-use rights. Tiebout's theory suggests residents' preferences for clean air can affect local public finance: if residents leave polluted places, the prospect of population and revenue losses may encourage local governments to improve environmental quality.

The study uses the unexpected release of the environmental documentary Under the Dome in early 2015 as a natural experiment. The authors use searches for its producer in 2011 as a proxy for local exposure and apply difference-in-differences; they report that a one-standard-deviation increase in exposure reduces land revenue by 21.45 billion yuan on average. They also use the minimum distance from a city to the producer's birthplace or first workplace as an instrument. Consistent with voting with one's feet, searches for housing prices in clean cities and local environmental investment increased after the documentary. About 100 faculty and students attended the 90-minute online seminar and discussion.
