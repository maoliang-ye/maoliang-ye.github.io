Prof. Ye Published at JEBO regarding Long-term Impacts of Early Adversity on Subjective Well-being

2025-02-04

      Recently, Associate Professor Ye Maoliang and co-authors Qianping Ren and Liyan Wang published a paper in the flagship journal of behavioral economics, the Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization (JEBO). The authors made equal contributions and are listed in alphabetical order by surname; Prof Ye serves as the correspondence author and a co-first author. 

       Abstract: Employing a difference-in-differences method across birth cohorts and regions with nationally representative data, this study examines the impact of the 1959–1961 Chinese Great Famine on survivors’ subjective well-being (SWB) fifty years later. Early-life exposure significantly reduces emotional and eudaimonic SWB, especially among females; evaluative SWB remains unaffected. Mechanism analysis highlights health status and social integration as primary channels, with socioeconomic status playing a limited role. This study is the first to systematically analyze the famine's SWB effects, revealing variability across well-being dimensions. Our findings underscore early-life circumstances’ pivotal role in SWB and the enduring consequences of adversity and public disasters.

        Link: https://authors.elsevier.com/c/1ihOj_3pQ3jXdO

      Cite:  ：Qianping Ren, Liyan Wang, Maoliang Ye. “Long-term impacts of early adversity on subjective well-being: Evidence from the Chinese great famine.” Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization, 230 (2025): 106905. 

       Behavioral economics and its applications in policy and management are core research areas for Professor Ye, while subjective well-being (SWB) is one fundamental topic of this fields.  Professor Ye has accumulated years of rich research experience on SWB and hosted MOE and NSFC grants. Besides JEBO, he published four related studies at top and well-known international journals including PNAS, Governance, Social Indicators Research, and will continue to conduct in-depth research in this area. His other behavioral economics studies (some of which interact with other fields) have published at Management Science, Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization, Journal of Comparative Economics, and European Journal of Political Economy.
